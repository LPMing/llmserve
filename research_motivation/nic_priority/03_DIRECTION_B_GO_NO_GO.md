# Direction B — go/no-go

## Decision

**Shared-NIC contention and policy sensitivity: observed in integrated
FlowSim. Direction B's full motivation gate: NOT PASSED.** Both rails have
87.29% time-weighted occupancy in the candidate P2D window. The event ledger
establishes a zero-TTFT-gain upper bound for request-targeted KV prioritizing
in this fixture, and EP_FIRST matches the EP_ISOLATED serving counterfactual.
A request-targeted flow-priority policy was not simulated, but no avoidable
TTFT/TBT loss beyond the measured class-level opportunity remains in this
workload.

On both independent 200-Gbps D0 receive rails, model-derived P2D demand plus
the recurrent EP4 group creates overlapping activity. Under FAIR, the link
is fully allocated during recorded active intervals and reaches 31.540 MiB
of analytical residual fluid backlog. In the 74.321-ms KV work window,
active intervals total 64.872 ms (87.29%); there are 207 event-driven busy
episodes, with maximum duration 1.522 ms. FAIR has 1.296 ms of simultaneous
KV+EP activity (1.74% of the window), so this is high-average-load,
recurrent-burst traffic rather than one uninterrupted saturation episode.
No Decode capacity admission wait is observed, so the measured serving
effect is network/collective related, not Decode max-sequence queueing.

The principal class-priority results are:

| Metric | FAIR | EP_FIRST | KV_FIRST |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Seed TPOT / mean ITL | 6.045367 ms | 6.008587 ms (-0.61%) | 8.733146 ms (+44.46%) |
| Seed pooled ITL P95 | 6.713704 ms | 6.647400 ms | 27.588228 ms |
| Seed pooled ITL P99 | 8.164016 ms | 8.164016 ms | 46.827701 ms |
| EP CCT mean | 11.031 us | 10.648 us | 39.029 us |
| EP CCT P99 | 12.464 us | 11.020 us | 1,326.375 us |
| Candidate TTFT, 2048-token | 74.468 ms | 74.468 ms | 74.468 ms |
| Candidate TTFT, 1024-token | 57.757 ms | 57.757 ms | 57.757 ms |

There are 8 seed requests and 184 pooled token intervals; intervals within a
request are correlated, so these are descriptive run statistics, not
independent-sample confidence intervals. There are 4,416 collective records;
the extreme KV_FIRST tail consists of a small minority of very delayed
collectives. Do not infer production frequency from this controlled run.

## Interpretation

KV_FIRST proves that a class policy can badly damage recurrent EP barriers
when it gives the P2D class precedence. But EP_FIRST removes nearly all of
that harm in this fixture: its TPOT gain over FAIR is only 0.61%, its
collective p99 is 11.02 us, and no TTFT penalty is measured for candidates.
EP_ISOLATED matches EP_FIRST for the key outcomes, which is consistent with
the small residual cross-class effect under FAIR. Because static EP_FIRST is
already effective and nearly free to candidate TTFT here, this experiment
does not motivate an additional request-specific or barrier-aware policy.

The `KV_FIRST` tail is not proof of physical switch priority inversion. It
is generated by FlowSim's traffic-class strict fluid allocation. A packet
backend test is needed to validate whether equivalent queue arbitration,
transport behavior, and completion tails occur on real RDMA semantics.

## Missing pass conditions

- No executed request-targeted priority replay or formal SLO-goodput oracle.
  The event-derived upper bound is limited to serving outcomes under this
  frozen workload and does not quantify how much per-request shard FCT could
  be reallocated.
- No B0/B1 isolated runs for this exact two-rail workload.
- No separate B4 scheduling replay; the offline bound is a critical-path
  impossibility result, not an alternative schedule.
- No packet-level or real-NIC priority validation.

The large KV_FIRST/EP_FIRST contrast therefore shows that a deliberately
unfavorable class ordering can hurt Decode in this FlowSim model. It does not
show that FAIR fails to protect urgent work enough to justify a new method:
FAIR→EP_FIRST improves mean seed TPOT by only 0.61%, and seed ITL p99 is
unchanged.

## Scope and stopping point

This is one controlled near-capacity, two-rail FlowSim point on a small
model-derived fixture. It is evidence for a basic class-priority tradeoff,
not a natural-workload prevalence claim. No more load scaling was performed
because this fixture already sits near its analytical load envelope; pushing
further would primarily demonstrate overload. The serving upper bound uses
the recorded event ordering, but request-level within-class flow FCT
reallocation was not tested.
